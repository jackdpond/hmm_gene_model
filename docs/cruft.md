Background
Genes and other regions of DNA encode information that is utilized within cells to produce RNA and sometimes proteins. These biomolecules act as components of a large dynamical system within cells, interacting to direct cellular processes in response to circumstances and stimuli. Genes and other regions of DNA can be regulated by cellular processes in this metaphorical program, resulting in increased or decreased transcription (conversion of DNA into RNA) of regulated DNA segments over time, with downstream effects on the cell’s future behavior.
Yeast cells can be grown under conditions that result in interesting periodic oscillations in cell activity known as “respiratory bursts”, alternating between the metabolic processes of glycolysis and respiration (Tu BP et al., 2005). Understanding how regions of DNA are differentially transcribed throughout this periodic metabolic cycle provides a fascinating window into the coordinated behavior of the cell.
Tu BP et al. used microarray data to measure transcription levels in yeast cells over the course of three of these metabolic cycles. Yeast cells were grown in a fermentor and synchronized in the respiratory burst cycle described above, and then every ~25 minutes, approximately 2x108 cells were extracted as a sample. For each sample, gene expression values were quantified as floats for over 6,400 yeast gene transcripts and other potentially meaningful RNA segments. The data consist of 36 time steps, each with expression values for the same set of over 9,000 features. Each transcript value for each RNA sequence is a float representing log intensity, which in microarrays is roughly proportional to the log of the sequence’s measured concentration.
The data is available via the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) database maintained by the National Center for Biotechnology Information, under the GEO accession code GSE3431. The scientific work resulting in the data was published in the paper. The authors performed some domain-specific normalization steps to the data. They then identified the most likely period of the vast majority of periodic transcription levels as ~300 minutes using an autocorrelation-based maximum likelihood analysis. Then k-nearest neighbors clustering was used to identify three distinct clusters of genes with different expression patterns across the period.

The quality of our results will be judged by a) how statistically likely our predicted hidden states are, b) how well our predicted states approximate conclusions made in established literature, and c) how well our models predict the trends anticipated in the original paper from which the data originated.
